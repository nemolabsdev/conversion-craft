# Experimentation & Measurement for Sales Pages

## Sources

- [Evan Miller — How Not to Run an A/B Test](https://www.evanmiller.org/how-not-to-run-an-ab-test.html) — quantifies the peeking problem (26.1% actual false-positive rate vs. stated 5%) and gives the fixed-sample-size formula n = 16(σ²/δ²).
- [Evan Miller — Simple Sequential A/B Testing](https://www.evanmiller.org/sequential-ab-testing.html) — concrete six-step sequential-testing procedure with explicit stopping thresholds (2√N one-sided, 2.25√N two-sided).
- [Optimizely — Fixed horizon, Bayesian, or sequential: choosing a stats engine](https://www.optimizely.com/field-notes/articles/how-to-choose-the-right-stats-engine) — explains how Stats Engine combines sequential testing with false-discovery-rate control to keep results valid at any peek.
- [Ronny Kohavi — Guardrail Metrics for A/B Tests (LinkedIn)](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/guardrail-metrics-ab-tests-ronny-kohavi) — defines guardrail metrics, splits them into trust-related vs. organizational, names Sample Ratio as the single most important guardrail.
- [Statsig — What are guardrail metrics in A/B tests?](https://www.statsig.com/blog/what-are-guardrail-metrics-in-ab-tests) — concrete guardrail examples (page load time, error rate, churn) and the "expect ~5% false-positive flicker" caution.
- [Analytics Toolkit — Sample Ratio Mismatch glossary](https://www.analytics-toolkit.com/glossary/sample-ratio-mismatch/) plus [Eppo/Microsoft threshold discussion](https://careersatdoordash.com/blog/addressing-the-challenges-of-sample-ratio-mismatch-in-a-b-testing/) — chi-square SRM detection and the p-value thresholds different platforms use (0.01 vs. 0.001 vs. 0.0005).
- [Convert — I don't have enough traffic to A/B test, now what?](https://www.convert.com/blog/a-b-testing/i-dont-have-enough-traffic-to-a-b-test-now-what/) — concrete minimum-traffic floor (≈1,000 sessions/variant/month, 30 conversions/variant/month) below which tests take months and results are unreliable.
- [GuessTheTest — Calculating sample size in A/B testing](https://guessthetest.com/calculating-sample-size-in-a-b-testing-everything-you-need-to-know/) — operational rule of thumb (30k visitors / 3k conversions per variant for high reliability), 2–8 week duration window tied to business cycles.
- [ProductLift / Umbrex — ICE Prioritization Framework](https://www.productlift.dev/blog/prioritizing-with-ice-model/) — Impact × Confidence × Ease scoring, Sean Ellis origin, 1–10 scale per factor.
- Search-verified aggregation of the CXL PXL framework (originated at [cxl.com/blog/better-way-prioritize-ab-tests](https://cxl.com/blog/better-way-prioritize-ab-tests/), cross-confirmed via independent secondary write-ups) — 10-question, mostly-binary scoring rubric designed to reduce the subjectivity of ICE.
- [Google Developers — Measure ecommerce (GA4)](https://developers.google.com/analytics/devguides/collection/ga4/ecommerce) — required/recommended parameters for `view_item`, `add_to_cart`, `begin_checkout`, `purchase`; items-array limits (200 items, 27 custom params).
- [Google Analytics Help — Default channel group](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/9756891) — official rule-based mapping from `utm_source`/`utm_medium` to channels (Organic Search, Paid Search, Email, Referral, Direct), and the exact Direct-channel match condition.
- [Growth Method — Object-Action Event Tracking Framework](https://growthmethod.com/object-action-framework/) plus [Amplitude — Event taxonomy](https://amplitude.com/explore/data/event-taxonomy) — past-tense Object-Action naming convention and tracking-plan governance practice.
- [Amplitude — Funnel analysis / drop-off](https://amplitude.com/explore/analytics/funnel-drop-off) — funnel-step definition, drop-off formula, and the "prioritize by absolute users lost, not percentage" rule.
- [Princeton CITP — No boundaries: exfiltration of personal data by session-replay scripts](https://blog.citp.princeton.edu/2017/11/15/no-boundaries-exfiltration-of-personal-data-by-session-replay-scripts/) — empirical finding: session-replay scripts on 482 of the Alexa top 50,000 sites leaked passwords, credit-card numbers, and health data despite masking attempts.
- [ppc.land — France's CNIL puts session replay tools under the privacy microscope](https://ppc.land/frances-cnil-puts-session-replay-tools-under-the-privacy-microscope/) — CNIL position that session replay cannot use the audience-measurement consent exemption and requires distinct, granular consent; draft recommendation under public consultation through April 2026.
- [Termly — What is Google Consent Mode v2?](https://termly.io/resources/articles/what-is-google-consent-mode-v2/) — mandatory since March 6, 2024 for sites serving ads to or measuring EU/EEA users; how denied consent degrades GA4/Ads data collection.

## Rules

1. Fix your total sample size and stopping point *before* launch using a power calculation — n = (Zα/2 + Zβ)² × [p1(1−p1) + p2(1−p2)] / (p1−p2)², or Evan Miller's shortcut n = 16(σ²/δ²) — and do not analyze the result until that N is reached. [LAW] (Evan Miller, How Not to Run an A/B Test)
2. If you check a fixed-horizon test repeatedly and stop the first time p < 0.05 appears, your actual false-positive rate is not 5% — in Miller's 150-observation simulation it reached 26.1%. Treat any "peeked and stopped early" result as invalid, not just optimistic. [LAW] (Evan Miller)
3. Halving your Minimum Detectable Effect requires roughly 4× the sample size (the relationship is quadratic in δ). Set MDE deliberately against how long you can actually run the test — do not silently shrink MDE to make a calculator output smaller. [FRAMEWORK] (Evan Miller / sample-size derivation)
4. If you need to monitor a test continuously rather than wait for a fixed N, use a named sequential procedure, not ad hoc peeking: fix N in advance, and stop for a winner when |T−C| reaches 2√N (one-sided) or 2.25√N (two-sided); stop with no winner when T+C reaches N. [FRAMEWORK] (Evan Miller, Simple Sequential A/B Testing)
5. Alternatively, run on a platform whose stats engine explicitly controls the false discovery rate under continuous looking (sequential + FDR control) rather than reporting raw fixed-horizon p-values as if they were still valid mid-test. [FRAMEWORK] (Optimizely Stats Engine)
6. Do not start an A/B test below roughly 1,000 sessions per variant per month and 30 conversions per variant per month on the page in question — below that floor, tests take months to reach power and the interim reads are noise, not signal. [FRAMEWORK] (Convert)
7. Below the traffic floor, replace the A/B test with qualitative instruments (session replay, moderated user testing, exit-intent surveys) to generate and de-risk hypotheses, then batch them into fewer, higher-MDE tests (large layout/offer changes, not micro-copy tweaks) once you do test. [FRAMEWORK] (Convert; GuessTheTest)
8. Run every test for a minimum of two full business cycles (commonly 2 weeks) even if significance is reached earlier, and cap most tests at 6–8 weeks to limit exposure to confounds like cookie churn and seasonality drift. [FRAMEWORK] (GuessTheTest)
9. Before trusting any result, run a Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM) check: a chi-square goodness-of-fit test comparing observed vs. intended variant allocation. Use p < 0.01 as a baseline alarm threshold; use a stricter p < 0.001–0.0005 if the cost of missing a broken randomizer is high (Microsoft and Eppo both run at this stricter end). [LAW] (Analytics Toolkit / Microsoft / Eppo)
10. Treat Sample Ratio as the mandatory, always-on guardrail metric on every experiment — an SRM flag invalidates the read regardless of how significant the primary metric looks, because it signals the randomization itself is broken. [FRAMEWORK] (Kohavi)
11. Define guardrail thresholds ("how bad is bad enough to roll back") in writing before the test starts — page-load-time delta, error-rate delta, unsubscribe/opt-out rate — so a mid-test guardrail dip is judged against a pre-committed bar, not a live debate. [FRAMEWORK] (Statsig; Kohavi)
12. Expect roughly 5% of monitored guardrail metrics to show "significant" movement by chance alone at any given check; do not roll back a test for a single guardrail flicker without confirming it persists or checking a second signal. [LAW] (Statsig)
13. Score test ideas with ICE (Impact × Confidence × Ease, each 1–10, product or average across the three) when you need a fast, lightweight shared vocabulary across a high-velocity growth team; expect the scores to be subjective and to drift by rater. [FRAMEWORK] (Sean Ellis / ProductLift)
14. When ICE's subjectivity becomes a problem (disagreement on scores, gaming), switch to PXL: score each hypothesis against a fixed ~10-question binary/near-binary rubric (e.g., above the fold = 1/0, noticeable within 5 seconds = 2/0, adds/removes an element rather than tweaks one = 2/0) instead of open 1–10 sliders. [FRAMEWORK] (CXL PXL)
15. Every PXL-scored hypothesis must cite which evidence source backs it (analytics funnel data, heatmap/mouse-tracking, qualitative feedback, or prior user testing) as one of the scored questions — a hypothesis with zero backing sources should score at the bottom of the queue regardless of how compelling it sounds. [FRAMEWORK] (CXL PXL)
16. Instrument the core purchase funnel with GA4's four standard ecommerce events — `view_item`, `add_to_cart`, `begin_checkout`, `purchase` — each carrying `currency`, `value`, and an `items` array, rather than inventing custom event names for the same actions. [FRAMEWORK] (Google Developers)
17. Give every `purchase` event a `transaction_id` that is unique per completed order; a reused or missing transaction_id causes double-counted or dropped revenue in funnel and revenue reports. [FRAMEWORK] (Google Developers)
18. Cap the `items` array at 200 line items and at most 27 custom parameters beyond the prescribed item fields (`item_id`, `item_name`, `price`, `item_brand`, `item_category`, `item_variant`) — designs that need more should aggregate at checkout, not push every line as a separate item field. [FRAMEWORK] (Google Developers)
19. Name events in past-tense Object-Action form (e.g., "Form Submitted," "Checkout Started," not "Submit Form" or "Track Checkout") so events read as things that already happened and stay consistent across implementers. [FRAMEWORK] (Segment/Amplitude Object-Action framework)
20. Maintain one authoritative tracking plan (schema registry or shared doc) that lists every approved event and property; new events require an entry there before they ship, not after. [FRAMEWORK] (Amplitude governance practice)
21. Define funnels as an explicit ordered sequence of the four core events (or your taxonomy's equivalent) and pick open vs. closed funnel type deliberately in your reporting tool — a closed funnel drops users who skip a step, an open funnel doesn't, and mixing the two across reports produces incomparable conversion rates. [FRAMEWORK] (Amplitude / GA4 Funnel Exploration)
22. When triaging funnel drop-off, prioritize by absolute users lost at a step, not the step's percentage drop-off — a 30% drop-off from 10,000 users is a bigger problem than a 50% drop-off from 500. [FRAMEWORK] (Amplitude)
23. Tag every external link that drives traffic to the sales page with at minimum `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign`; never tag internal links, since doing so overwrites the visitor's original acquisition source in session-scoped reporting. [FRAMEWORK] (Google Campaign URL Builder guidance)
24. Standardize all UTM parameter values to lowercase, hyphen-separated, and documented in a shared naming-convention sheet — GA4's Default Channel Grouping match is not itself case-sensitive, but the underlying source/medium *dimension* report treats "Email" and "email" as distinct rows, fragmenting acquisition data. [FRAMEWORK] (industry UTM governance practice; Google channel-grouping docs)
25. Know the exact official match rule for the Direct channel so you can diagnose "Direct" inflation: source exactly `(direct)` AND medium is one of `(not set)` / `(none)` — any dark-traffic or missing-UTM link lands here by default. [LAW] (Google Analytics Help, Default channel group)
26. Treat heatmaps and session replay as hypothesis-generating qualitative evidence only — they show *where* users hesitate or misclick but not reliably *why*; never ship a change on heatmap evidence alone without a confirming quantitative test once you're above the traffic floor (rule 6). [FRAMEWORK] (CRO evidence-stack practice)
27. Configure session-replay masking to whitelist safe fields rather than blacklist sensitive ones — default masking that only excludes password/credit-card selectors has been shown to still leak addresses, CVVs, and health data on production sites; audit every form on the page against the tool's mask list before launch. [LAW] (Princeton CITP study: 482/50,000 top sites leaked data despite masking attempts)
28. Verify session-replay masking with a manual pass per template (checkout, account, contact forms), not just trust on the vendor's default exclude classes (`.hotjar-mask`, `.fs-mask`, etc.) — those only cover fields the vendor could anticipate, not your custom form markup. [FRAMEWORK] (Princeton CITP; vendor masking docs)
29. Implement Google Consent Mode v2 on any page that serves Google Ads or GA4 to EU/EEA visitors — it has been effectively mandatory since March 6, 2024, and without it, GA4/Ads stop collecting data on new EU/EEA users entirely. [LAW] (Termly / Google Consent Mode v2 requirement)
30. Do not fold session-replay consent into your general analytics consent toggle — regulatory guidance (CNIL) treats session replay as a distinct processing purpose requiring its own granular consent, and it does not qualify for the "anonymous audience measurement" consent exemption that some analytics tools use. [LAW] (CNIL / ppc.land)
31. If you rely on a consent exemption to run analytics without a cookie banner, confirm all four CNIL conditions simultaneously: the tool measures site/app performance only, produces anonymous statistics only, is not combined with other data systems, and does not enable cross-site tracking — losing any one condition requires falling back to consent. [LAW] (CNIL analytics exemption criteria)
32. [TREND-2026] Track CNIL's draft session-replay recommendation (public consultation running through April 2026) before finalizing a session-replay consent flow — the final rule is expected to tighten default-off requirements for session replay specifically, ahead of and stricter than general analytics consent norms. [TREND-2026] (ppc.land / CNIL consultation)

## Anti-patterns

- **Peek-and-ship**: checking a fixed-horizon test daily and calling it the moment p crosses 0.05, then treating that p-value as if it still means 5% error — it doesn't (rule 2).
- **Underpowered micro-tests on low-traffic pages**: running a two-week test on a page with a few hundred sessions/week and declaring a "20% lift" winner that is actually noise (rules 1, 6, 8).
- **Metric fishing**: testing without a pre-declared primary metric, then scanning a dashboard of a dozen metrics post-hoc and reporting whichever one crossed significance as "the result."
- **Ignoring SRM**: reading a lift as real when the variant split itself is broken (e.g., 52/48 instead of 50/50 at scale) — the randomizer failure invalidates everything downstream (rules 9–10).
- **No guardrails, or guardrails defined after the fact**: shipping a "winning" checkout variant that quietly doubled page load time or error rate because no one was watching until launch (rule 11).
- **Treating ICE/PXL scores as objective truth**: letting a single loud stakeholder's "9/10 confidence" dominate a queue, or reverse-engineering scores to justify a pet idea already decided on.
- **UTM-tagging internal navigation**: putting `utm_source=header` on an internal nav link, which resets the visitor's session attribution mid-journey and corrupts acquisition reporting (rule 23).
- **Heatmap-as-proof**: shipping a redesign because "the heatmap showed people weren't seeing the CTA" without ever A/B testing the fix against the original (rule 26).
- **Default-trusting session-replay masking**: enabling a session-replay tool on a checkout page and assuming its default password/credit-card exclusions are sufficient, without auditing custom fields (rules 27–28).
- **Bundling session-replay consent into a generic "Accept Analytics" toggle**: creates regulatory exposure because replay is a distinct, higher-scrutiny processing purpose (rule 30).
- **Statistical significance mistaken for practical significance**: a 0.3-percentage-point lift on a huge sample reads as "significant" but may not be worth the implementation or maintenance cost — significance answers "is this effect real," not "is this effect worth shipping."
- **Running a test through a known confound window** (e.g., a single promotional weekend, a site outage, a major traffic-source change) and reporting the result as generalizable.

## Open questions

- How should fixed-horizon vs. sequential testing guidance change for teams adopting AI-driven personalization or multi-armed bandits on a sales page, where the "test" never really stops rather than reaching a fixed N?
- What is the right minimal guardrail-metric set specifically for a single-page, single-step checkout (vs. the multi-page product/app guardrails most published guidance assumes)?
- With Consent Mode v2 modeling gaps for denied-consent EU/EEA traffic, what error margin on modeled conversions is acceptable before a sales-page A/B test's sample size math (rule 1) is itself compromised by missing data?
- Where exactly is the defensible line between "heatmap/session-replay evidence is sufficient to ship" and "must be validated with an A/B test," for teams permanently below the minimum traffic floor (rule 6) who can never reach a fixed-horizon test's required N?
- CNIL's session-replay recommendation is still a draft under consultation (rule 32) — how much should a page's consent architecture be built ahead of the final rule vs. wait for it, given the compliance cost of re-architecting consent flows twice?
