## Rules

**Product page anatomy**

1. Render the primary product image, product name, price, and a visible add-to-cart/buy CTA inside the first viewport with zero scrolling, on both desktop and mobile — this is the baseline "above the fold" checklist across the research set. [LAW] — Baymard, Kissmetrics
2. Stock a minimum of 5 product images/angles per product page; pages with 5+ images convert roughly 60% higher than pages with a single image (two independent sources agree on this figure). [LAW] — Kissmetrics, Command C
3. Assume the visitor's first action on any PDP is to browse the image gallery before reading any text (56% of users, measured) — put gallery navigation (arrows, dots, swipe affordance) in an obvious, immediately visible spot, not below other content. [LAW] — Command C (citing Baymard)
4. Support pinch-to-zoom and double-tap-to-zoom on every product image on mobile; roughly 40% of ecommerce sites currently fail to support this, and it is a documented cause of frustration and abandonment. [LAW] — Command C
5. Test and fix landscape-orientation image scaling specifically: 52% of retailers fail to scale product images proportionally in landscape, and 50% of users who find images too small will physically rotate their phone to compensate rather than pinch-zoom. [LAW] — Command C
6. Build the mobile gallery as swipeable/gesture-driven, not a small hero image with a row of static thumbnails underneath — the thumbnail-strip pattern is a documented mobile failure mode. [LAW] — Command C
7. Display shipping cost/timeframe and return-policy information near the buy button on the product page itself, not only inside the cart or checkout — unexpected shipping costs are the single most-cited cause of cart abandonment across two independent surveys (40% of non-browsing abandoners per Baymard; 48% per Kissmetrics). [LAW] — Baymard, Kissmetrics
8. Show free-shipping eligibility directly on the product page when applicable; this single line of copy is associated with a 7–12% lift in add-to-cart rate. [LAW] — Kissmetrics
9. Make a visible return policy part of the product page (not a footer link only) — pages with a visible return policy convert 5–15% higher than pages without one. [LAW] — Kissmetrics
10. Place trust content (guarantee copy, "easy returns," security/payment badges) physically adjacent to the CTA, not in a page footer — 57% of researched sites already place returns/guarantee messaging near the buy button as a deliberate risk-reversal tactic. [LAW] — Crazy Egg
11. Surface review count and average rating near the price/CTA cluster, and consider labeling verified purchases — 67% of researched sites use verified-purchase labels as a credibility signal against fake-review skepticism. [TREND-2026] — Crazy Egg
12. Treat visible customer reviews as a major conversion lever, not decoration — products with visible reviews show +270% conversion for everyday goods and +380% for higher-priced/higher-consideration items in the cited comparison. [LAW] — Crazy Egg

**Checkout — form design and field count**

13. Audit and cut checkout form fields aggressively: the average US checkout ships 23.48 form elements by default (confirmed independently by two sources), against which 20–60% are typically removable through merging fields (e.g., first/last name into one control) and hiding optional fields behind progressive disclosure. [LAW] — Baymard, Shopify
14. Mark required fields explicitly rather than only flagging optional ones — when only optional fields were labeled, 32% of test participants still skipped a required field. Don't rely on an implied "everything else is required" convention. [LAW] — Baymard
15. Use single-column checkout layouts, not multi-column forms — single-column reduces field-skipping and misordered completion. [LAW] — NN/g, Shopify
16. Use top-aligned labels on checkout form fields rather than left- or right-aligned labels; top alignment keeps label and input on one visual axis and completes faster. Confidence note: this specific claim is a long-standing, widely-cited NN/g finding but was not visible verbatim on the specific report page fetched this pass — treat as high-confidence but not freshly re-verified. [LAW] — NN/g
17. Validate each field inline, triggered on blur (when the user leaves the field) — not on the first keystroke and not only at submit. Once an error has been shown, clear it live as the user corrects it, at the keystroke level. [LAW] — Baymard
18. Do not ship checkout with zero inline validation — 31% of sites currently have none at all, and a further 4% of the sites that do implement it get the interaction wrong (premature or non-clearing errors). [LAW] — Baymard
19. Keep error messages inline and non-intrusive to layout — an error must not cause content reflow that hides other fields or pushes the CTA out of view. [LAW] — Baymard
20. Offer address autocomplete/type-ahead lookup so a shopper can type a few characters and have the rest of the address fill itself; treat this as baseline expectation rather than a premium feature by 2026. [TREND-2026] — Shopify

**Checkout — flow, guest access, and payment**

21. Make guest checkout the default and single most prominent path on the checkout entry screen — 62% of sites currently fail to make it the most prominent option, and forced account creation is independently cited as a reason for abandonment (18% per Baymard's checkout-UX data; corroborated by Shopify at 19%). [LAW] — Baymard, Shopify
22. Show a step/progress indicator on any checkout that spans more than one page or screen — users abandon less when they can see how much of the flow remains. [LAW] — NN/g, Shopify
23. Expose digital wallets (Apple Pay, Google Pay, PayPal, Shop Pay, etc.) as distinct, clearly labeled one-tap buttons positioned above the manual card-entry form — wallets accounted for 56% of global ecommerce spend in 2025, making them the majority payment behavior, not an edge case. [TREND-2026] — Shopify
24. Plan for one-tap biometric checkout (Face ID/Touch ID-backed wallet payment) as the primary mobile path rather than a bolt-on option; this is converging into a 2026 baseline expectation across multiple sources, though the underlying market-share figures could not be independently re-verified this pass. [TREND-2026] — Shopify; consistent with (but not independently confirmed against) BTNG and CXL scout claims
25. Size every checkout touch target at a 44–48px minimum (Apple HIG / Material Design floor) and match on-screen keyboard type to field type — numeric pad for card number/CVC/ZIP, email keyboard for email address. [LAW] — Shopify (citing Apple/Google HIG)
26. Reassure on payment security directly next to the payment-method fields — security/fraud concern about card data is independently cited as an abandonment cause by both Baymard (19% of non-browsing abandoners) and Shopify (19% trust-related abandonment). [LAW] — Baymard, Shopify
27. Treat checkout redesign as one of the highest-leverage fixes available: Baymard's research associates comprehensive checkout-UX improvement with an approximate 35% potential conversion increase, and the average audited site needs roughly 32 distinct fixes to get there — this is not a one-tweak change. [LAW] — Baymard

**Cart abandonment — causes to design against**

28. Design against a realistic ~70% baseline cart-abandonment rate (70.19–70.22% depending on the specific Baymard dataset cited, compiled from 50 studies spanning 2006–2025) — the goal of PDP/checkout fixes is to move the fixable share of this number, not to eliminate abandonment entirely. [LAW] — Baymard
29. Treat "just browsing / not ready to buy" as a separate, non-addressable ~42% of all abandonment — exclude it when calculating the ROI of a specific UX fix, since no amount of PDP or checkout optimization converts a browsing-stage visitor. [LAW] — Baymard
30. Prioritize fixes in this order of cited impact among addressable (non-browsing) abandonment causes: unexpected costs (~40% of non-browsers per Baymard, ~48% of general abandoners per Kissmetrics) > slow/unclear delivery (~20%) > payment security concerns (~19%) > forced account creation (~18%) > checkout complexity/length (~17%) > site errors (~17%) > return-policy dissatisfaction (~13%) > total cost not visible upfront (~12%) > card decline (~10%) > insufficient payment options (~9%). [LAW] — Baymard
31. Show the running total — including estimated shipping and tax — as early as the cart page, before checkout begins, since "unable to see total cost upfront" is independently cited as a distinct abandonment driver from "unexpected costs" generally. [LAW] — Baymard

**Mobile commerce**

32. Budget extra design and QA attention for mobile checkout specifically: mobile checkout is consistently rated worse than desktop across the research set (63% of mobile sites rated mediocre-or-worse checkout UX vs. 64% desktop — nearly identical — but mobile apps improve to 46% mediocre-or-worse, suggesting native app UX patterns currently outperform mobile web). [LAW] — Baymard
33. Expect materially lower mobile conversion rates than desktop as a planning baseline, and treat a mobile-specific fix (e.g., wallet checkout, autofill, larger tap targets) as higher-leverage per hour of design work than the equivalent desktop fix. Confidence note: the exact percentage gap (commonly cited as roughly half, e.g., ~1.5–2.2% mobile vs. ~3.3–3.9% desktop) could not be independently verified via direct source fetch this pass — see Open Questions. [TREND-2026] — NN/g, BTNG (both unverified via direct fetch)

**Testing discipline (GoodUI)**

34. Do not treat any "proven" pattern — including the ones in this file — as guaranteed to win on your own traffic; validate with your own A/B test before rolling out broadly. Even GoodUI's own curated, previously-successful pattern library does not win every re-test when applied to a new site. [TREND-2026] — GoodUI
35. Reduce the number of options presented at any single decision point (plan tiers, shipping options, color/size variants shown at once) — this is among the most consistently tested winning patterns in GoodUI's public pattern library (13 patterns tagged "choice"). [LAW] — GoodUI
36. Size and place CTA buttons using Fitts's Law logic — larger target, shorter distance from the user's current focus/thumb position, higher contrast against surrounding elements — rather than relying on copy alone to drive clicks. [LAW] — GoodUI

## Trends 2024-2026

- **Digital wallets overtaking manual card entry as the primary payment method.** Wallets (Apple Pay, Google Pay, PayPal, Shop Pay, etc.) reached 56% of global ecommerce spend in 2025 (Shopify). **Shelf life: durable, multi-year** — this is a structural payment-behavior shift, not a seasonal fad; expect wallet-first checkout design to keep displacing card-first design through the late 2020s.
- **One-tap biometric checkout becoming a baseline expectation rather than a premium feature.** Face ID/Touch ID-backed wallet payment is converging toward "must-have" status on mobile checkout flows. **Shelf life: durable** — tracks the wallet-adoption trend above and the underlying hardware (biometric sensors) is now near-universal on smartphones.
- **Address autofill/autocomplete moving from differentiator to baseline.** Type-ahead address lookup is now framed as a default expectation, not a feature to sell. **Shelf life: short as a "trend," about to become a [LAW]** — likely indistinguishable from table stakes within 1–2 years; revisit this file's tagging then.
- **Verified-purchase / authenticated-review labeling as a trust-signal standard.** 67% of researched sites already label verified purchases, a direct response to fake-review skepticism. **Shelf life: durable**, and likely to strengthen further given regulatory pressure on fake reviews (e.g., FTC rules) in several major markets.
- **Checkout-step compression (multi-page toward single-page/accordion flows).** Consistent multi-source direction (Baymard's field-reduction findings, Shopify's guidance, and the scout's CXL claim of single-page outperforming multi-step) toward fewer, denser checkout screens with progressive disclosure rather than sequential pages. **Shelf life: durable** but the specific per-step dropoff percentage in this file's Rule set (CXL-sourced) is unverified — treat the direction as durable, the exact number as provisional.

## Anti-patterns

- **Hiding total cost (shipping, tax, fees) until the final checkout step.** This is the single largest documented cause of cart abandonment across two independently-sourced surveys (Baymard, Kissmetrics). Any "surprise" cost revealed late in the flow directly taxes conversion — show the estimate as early as the cart page.
- **Forcing account creation before purchase, or making it appear mandatory.** Independently cited as an abandonment driver by Baymard (18%) and Shopify (19%). Guest checkout must be the default, most-prominent option, with account creation offered only after (or entirely separate from) purchase completion.
- **Marking only optional fields and leaving required fields unmarked.** Counter-intuitively, this convention causes more required-field errors (32% skip rate in Baymard's testing) than explicitly marking required fields — the implicit "everything else is required" assumption does not reliably transfer to users.
- **Validating on every keystroke, or only at submit.** Keystroke-level validation before the user has finished typing produces false-positive errors and feels punitive; submit-only validation delays feedback until the user has already moved on mentally. On-blur validation with live error-clearing is the tested middle ground.
- **Static thumbnail strips under a small hero image on mobile product pages.** A documented mobile failure pattern — mobile galleries need to be swipeable/gesture-driven, and roughly 40% of sites still lack basic pinch/double-tap zoom, compounding the problem.
- **A suspiciously perfect 5.0-star rating with no critical reviews.** The qualitative principle that some visible negative/mixed reviews build more trust than a flawless rating is broadly supported by conversion-research literature; however, the specific "4.2–4.7 outperforms 5.0" figure attributed to Crazy Egg in the scout's source list could not be confirmed on the actual page content during this research pass — see Open Questions before using this as a hard numeric rule.
- **Award-circuit visual patterns applied to sales/checkout flows.** Consistent with the finding already documented in this curriculum's `layout-spacing.md` (scroll-jacking, heavy hero animation, experimental navigation, delayed content reveal) — these patterns push price, CTA, and trust signals below the fold and add load time, directly contradicting the above-the-fold and speed-sensitive rules in this file. A design-award-winning ecommerce site and a high-converting one are frequently different sites; validate award-inspired patterns against your own funnel before reuse, exactly as with layout/spacing patterns generally.
- **Treating GoodUI's (or any single source's) "winning pattern" as self-validating.** A pattern library built from other companies' A/B tests is a hypothesis generator, not a guarantee for your specific product, audience, or price point.
