## 0. Offer architecture (named per Rule 9, before anything else)

| Element | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Lead offer** | Email capture ("get the spec sheet / notify me") — for visitors not ready to buy. Free, low-commitment. |
| **Core offer** | AURI A1 purchase, $349, one-time payment, free shipping, 30-day returns, 2-year warranty. |
| **Order bump / upsell** | **None in scope.** Brief defines a single SKU at a single price point. Do not invent a bundle, accessory upsell, or second tier to justify an anchoring trick (Rule 13/26) — there is no second tier to anchor against. If one is added later, it must be logged as a scope change here first. |
| **Primary conversion goal** | Add to cart / purchase. **Single** primary CTA. |
| **Secondary offer** | Email capture — visually subordinate (secondary button style, smaller, never competing weight with "Add to Cart"). |

## 1. Positioning — Dunford 5-step canvas

**Step 2 pressure-test applied to Step 1 (Rule 2):** the brief names two alternatives (Sony/Bose flagship, $150 "good enough" tier). That's short of a real alternatives list — it omits status quo. Added below.

| # | Step | Content |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Competitive alternatives** | What the visitor does instead if AURI A1 didn't exist | **(a) Status quo / do nothing** — keep using the sub-$150 headphones already owned, despite being disappointed by them (explicitly stated in brief: "already own cheaper headphones and are disappointed"). This is the highest-volume alternative and the one most positioning exercises skip. **(b) Sony/Bose flagship** (WH-1000XM5-class, Bose QuietComfort Ultra-class), $399–449. **(c) Re-buy at the "good enough" tier** (~$150) — repeat the same purchase that already disappointed them, because it's cheap and familiar. |
| **2. Unique attributes** | What AURI A1 has that none of the three alternatives have | Adaptive ANC that learns the individual user's specific distraction profile (vs. fixed presets on Sony/Bose, vs. no meaningful ANC on the $150 tier). 60-hour battery — roughly double the flagship ANC class. Titanium drivers with studio tuning. Repairable design: swappable parts + a 10-year support commitment. |
| **3. Value (attribute → outcome)** | Translated to what the customer actually gets | Adaptive ANC → silence tuned to *your* environment, not a generic mode you have to hunt through. 60h battery → you charge on your schedule, never mid-deep-work-block. Titanium/studio tuning → sound accurate enough for critical listening without fatigue, not consumer bass-boosted EQ. Repairable + 10-yr support → this is the last headphone purchase for a decade, not the third $150 pair you'll throw out. |
| **4. Who cares most** | Best-fit customer, specific | Remote/hybrid professionals, 28–45, whose work requires sustained deep focus (writing, coding, analysis, back-to-back calls). Trigger event: current cheap ANC headphones are actively failing them — battery dying mid-session, ANC not blocking the *specific* sound that breaks their concentration, cheap build feeling disposable. They've already spent money once on this problem and got burned; they're now evaluating whether to spend $150 again or step up. |
| **5. Market category** | The frame that makes the value obvious on first read | **"Premium ANC headphones built for deep focus"** — not just "wireless headphones" (swap test: too broad, headline "The wireless headphones for professionals who want silence" would fit any Bluetooth headphone) and not just "titanium-driver headphones" (swap test: too narrow, becomes a spec sheet, loses the outcome). "Deep-focus ANC headphone" keeps Sony/Bose as the direct comparison set (correct — that's who the audience is actually cross-shopping) while making the durability/adaptive angle the wedge. |

**Reconciled positioning statement:**

> For remote and hybrid professionals who depend on real silence to do focused work — and who already tried a cheaper ANC headphone and got burned — AURI A1 is the premium ANC headphone that learns what specifically distracts you, runs 60 hours between charges, and is built to be repaired for a decade instead of replaced every 12–18 months. You get Sony/Bose-class silence and sound without repeating the $150 mistake, and without paying for two years of upgrade-cycle marketing you won't use.

## 2. Awareness stage → what the hero must say first

**Stage (from brief): Solution-Aware.** The visitor already knows they want ANC headphones; they don't know AURI exists. This is *not* Unaware/Problem-Aware (skip problem-education — they're already sold on "I need ANC headphones") and *not* Product-Aware/Most-Aware (don't assume they already know and prefer AURI specifically — don't lead purely with price/offer).

Per copy-chief Rule 1 and marketing-strategy Rule 6, a Solution-Aware hero must **name the solution category + AURI's edge within it** — not educate about the problem, not open with the price.

**The hero's line 1 must therefore do three things at once:**
1. Confirm this is the category they're already shopping (ANC / deep-focus headphone) — no re-education needed.
2. Immediately differentiate from the two things they're mentally comparing it to: the Sony/Bose flagship they're pricing against, and the cheap pair already disappointing them.
3. Carry the One Thing to Remember (Section 3) — because 80–90% of visitors read only headline + CTA (copy-chief Rule 3).

**What it must NOT do:** open with a problem-education hook ("Tired of noisy offices?") — wasted words on a visitor who already knows this; open with price/offer alone — too Most-Aware, skips the "why AURI and not Sony/Bose" question they're actively holding.

**Assumption logged (Rule 8):** this structure assumes solution-aware traffic (organic/comparison search, category-intent) is the dominant source, per the brief. If cold/paid awareness traffic is added to this same URL later, the page should default to the colder problem-first structure and let solution/product-aware visitors self-select down via the comparison section — that is a deliberate future tradeoff, not today's build.

## 3. Message hierarchy (handoff to Copy Chief)

**One Thing to Remember** (passes the headline test — Rule 17: tells you what's sold and to whom; not pasteable onto a competitor's page unchanged, since Sony/Bose don't credibly offer 10-year repairability):

> **AURI A1 is the ANC headphone built to last a decade, not a year — with noise cancelling that learns what's actually distracting you.**

**Three Supporting Proofs** (each passes the specificity test — Rule 16: number, named mechanism, or named comparison, not an adjective):

1. **Named mechanism:** Adaptive ANC that learns your specific distraction profile over time — not a fixed factory preset like the modes on Sony/Bose flagships.
2. **Number + named comparison:** 60-hour battery life — roughly double the ANC flagship class (Sony/Bose land in the 24–30 hour range with ANC on; confirm current published specs before this goes on the comparison table).
3. **Number + named comparison:** Swappable parts backed by a 10-year support commitment — versus the $150 "good enough" headphones already being replaced every 12–18 months.

**One Action:**

> Add AURI A1 to cart — $349, free shipping, 30-day returns.

(Secondary, subordinate action for not-ready visitors: capture email in exchange for the spec sheet / launch notification — never equal visual weight to the primary CTA.)

## 4. Objection sequence for a $349 headphone purchase

Cognitive order per marketing-strategy Rule 19 / copy-chief Rule 15. Each row is what *this page specifically* says — not a generic answer.

| # | Objection | What the visitor is thinking | What this page says |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Legitimacy** | "Is this real? Who is AURI?" | Real product photography (no stock), the 2-year warranty + 30-day return terms stated in plain language above the fold, and the 10-year support commitment itself doubles as a legitimacy signal — a company doesn't promise a decade of parts support as a marketing line without backing it operationally. |
| **2. Relevance** | "Does this work for MY situation — I already own headphones, I already tried ANC." | Explicit persona language: "built for people who need real silence to do real work," scenario copy naming open-plan offices, back-to-back calls, and WFH distractions — directly acknowledging the visitor already owns a disappointing pair rather than pretending this is their first purchase. |
| **3. Mechanism** | "How does adaptive ANC actually work? What's a titanium driver doing for me?" | Plain-language explanation of the learning ANC (what it listens for, how it adapts over the first days of use, contrasted with a fixed preset) and what "studio tuning" concretely means (frequency response tuned for accuracy, not a consumer V-shaped EQ curve) plus how the repair/swap process works in practice. |
| **4. Results** | "Are the numbers real, or marketing?" | A checkable spec comparison: AURI A1 vs. Sony/Bose flagship vs. the $150 tier, side by side — battery hours, ANC performance, driver material — sourced to current published competitor specs, not rounded or vague. |
| **5. Peer validation** | "What do people who bought this actually say?" | Named, attributed testimonials (real name, title/company, photo) from the target persona — ideally including at least one "switched from [Sony/Bose model]" and one "switched from a cheap pair" quote — plus review count and average rating surfaced near price/CTA. |
| **6. Pricing / terms transparency** | "What am I actually paying for, and is $349 fair?" | Price shown plainly on-page as a round number ($349, never gated behind "contact us," never .99-dressed — round pricing matches the premium category per Rule 25). What's included is stated directly beside the price: free shipping, 2-year warranty, 30-day returns, no subscription. |
| **7. Risk** | "What if I get burned again, like I did with my last pair?" | Risk-reversal copy placed directly beside/below the final CTA (never buried only in FAQ): 30-day returns spelled out with the actual mechanic (how to return, who pays shipping), 2-year warranty terms, and the repairability/10-year support promise reframed here as *the* answer to "what if this breaks in year 3" — the exact fear a previously-disappointed buyer has. |

## 5. Section order

Default order per marketing-strategy Rule 7, adjusted for solution-aware traffic and the Rule 19/20/21 objection map (every section assigned to exactly one objection number or to the offer/CTA; legitimacy+relevance above the fold; mechanism+results mid-page; risk beside the final CTA).

| Order | Section | Maps to | What it must accomplish |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Nav** | Offer/CTA | Persistent access to the primary CTA and price; no navigation away from the conversion path. |
| 2 | **Hero** | Offer/CTA | Names the category + AURI's edge for a solution-aware reader (Section 2); carries the One Thing to Remember (Section 3) in the headline; houses the primary CTA. This is the 80–90%-read section — it must work with zero scroll. |
| 3 | **Trust / proof bar** | Objection 1 — legitimacy | Aggregate rating + review count, warranty/returns/shipping badges, one short named testimonial — concrete, checkable trust signals, above the fold, distinct from the deeper testimonials later (no redundant proof stacking). |
| 4 | **"Built for deep focus" relevance section** | Objection 2 — relevance | Names the specific visitor and their specific prior disappointment; makes clear this page knows who's reading it before asking for anything else. |
| 5 | **Secondary CTA (email capture)** | Offer/CTA (secondary) | Catches not-ready visitors without competing visually with the primary Add to Cart CTA. |
| 6 | **Mechanism section** (adaptive ANC + titanium drivers/studio tuning — 2 feature blocks) | Objection 3 — mechanism | Explains how the two hero-level differentiators actually work, each as a benefit-headed block (FAB ladder: feature → advantage → benefit) with a real product image, not stock art. |
| 7 | **Results / comparison section** (60h battery number + AURI vs. Sony/Bose vs. $150-tier spec table) | Objection 4 — results | Makes every claim checkable — a side-by-side table beats an adjective every time here. |
| 8 | **Peer validation section** | Objection 5 — peer validation | Named, attributed testimonials/case-style quotes, ideally including a switch-from-Sony/Bose and a switch-from-cheap-pair quote, since those map directly to the two competitive alternatives named in Section 1. |
| 9 | **Pricing + repairability/value section** | Objection 6 — pricing/terms transparency | States $349 plainly with inclusions listed beside it; uses the 10-year-support/repairability story to justify the price against both alternatives (cheaper-but-disposable, and pricier-but-not-repairable). |
| 10 | **Final CTA + risk-reversal band** | Objection 7 — risk + Offer/CTA | Restates 30-day returns and 2-year warranty directly beside the button, not only in the FAQ; this is the last thing before the ask. |
| 11 | **FAQ** | Residual objections 1–7, one entry per numbered objection only | Each entry must trace to a specific objection from Section 4's table (e.g., "How does the return process work?" → risk; "How long does adaptive ANC take to learn my environment?" → mechanism). No generic company-info filler entries. |
| 12 | **Footer** | — | Legal, policy links, utility. Not a persuasion section. |

Total feature/benefit blocks (Section 6 + 7 combined): 4 — within the 3–6 cap (Rule 22).

## 6. What NOT to include (scope discipline)

- **No second pricing tier or bundle.** The offer architecture (Section 0) defines one SKU at one price. Do not build a decoy/anchor tier (Rule 13/14) — there is nothing in the brief to anchor against, and inventing one is scope creep, not strategy.
- **No fabricated "was $X, now $349" strikethrough price.** There is no evidence of a prior list price in the brief; a fake anchor is a legitimacy risk (reopens Objection 1), not a proof asset.
- **No countdown timers or fake scarcity** ("only 3 left," resets on refresh) — explicitly forbidden by copy-chief; the brand's entire pitch is durability/trust, and fake urgency directly undercuts it.
- **No autoplaying testimonial carousel** — breaks readable proof (copy-chief Rule 19/anti-patterns); use a static grid or hover/tap-controlled rotation.
- **No blog/content-marketing material embedded on this page.** SEO/educational content belongs on a separate page; this is a single-conversion sales page and every section must earn its place per Rule 18.
- **No generic "About Us" / company timeline section.** If a legitimacy-boosting founder/team credential exists, fold one line into the trust bar (Objection 1) — do not give it its own section; it doesn't map to any objection on its own.
- **No live chat widget or comparison-shopping tool** as a default build item — not requested in the brief, adds a second interaction path competing with the single primary CTA (Rule 10/13).
- **No subscription, membership, or recurring-payment language.** This is a one-time $349 purchase — say so plainly in the pricing section to preempt a subscription assumption.
- **No stock photography of generic people wearing headphones.** Every supporting image must be the real product (Rule 9/22); stock art fails the feature-block requirement outright.
- **No spec-dump jargon replacing benefit language.** "Titanium drivers" needs its benefit stated in the same breath (Section 4, mechanism); raw spec lists belong only inside the comparison table (Objection 4), not carrying the narrative.
- **No account creation or extra form fields before purchase.** Checkout stays to the minimum fields for the transaction (copy-chief Rule 33); the only other form on the page is the single-field email capture (secondary offer).
- **No second competing "primary" CTA style.** Every button on the page besides "Add to Cart" (including the email capture) must be visually subordinate — smaller, lower-contrast, or text-link weight.
